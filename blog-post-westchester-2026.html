Is It a Good Time to Buy a House in Westchester County, NY? Here's What I'm Seeing in 2026

Posted in 2026 by Connie Stetler

If you're thinking about buying a house in Westchester County, NY, you've probably asked yourself at least one of these questions: Should I wait for interest rates to drop? Will home prices come down? Is the housing market going to crash? Would I be better off waiting until next year?

I hear these questions all the time. And after decades of working in real estate throughout Westchester County, I understand why buyers are hesitant.

But here's something I want you to consider.

While so many buyers are waiting for the market to change, opportunities may be passing them by.

Right now, I'm seeing buyers sitting on the sidelines, waiting for lower mortgage rates, more inventory, or a significant drop in home prices. Meanwhile, some of the buyers who are actively looking are finding less competition, more properties to consider, and even opportunities to negotiate.

So, is now a good time to buy a home in Westchester County?

My answer is yes, provided you're financially prepared and you find the right house.

Let me explain why.

Why Waiting to Buy a House in Westchester County Could Mean Missing an Opportunity

One of the biggest misconceptions I encounter is that there's going to be some magical moment when interest rates drop, home prices fall, and buyers suddenly have their pick of properties.

Wouldn't that be nice?

The problem is that nobody can predict exactly when that moment will arrive, or whether it will happen at all.

And there's something else to consider.

When mortgage rates decline, buyers who have been waiting may jump back into the market. That could mean more competition for the same homes, potentially creating bidding wars and pushing prices higher again.

On the other hand, when fewer buyers are actively looking, sellers may be more willing to negotiate.

That's what makes the current market interesting.

In some Westchester communities and price ranges, I'm seeing opportunities that weren't necessarily available when buyers were competing aggressively for nearly every new listing.

That doesn't mean every property is a bargain. Far from it.

But it does mean buyers should be paying attention rather than automatically putting their plans on hold.

Westchester County Is Not Your Average Real Estate Market

If you've lived in Westchester County for any length of time, you probably already know that our housing market doesn't always follow national real estate trends.

I like to think of Westchester as its own little real estate bubble.

We're just outside New York City, with access to major employment centers throughout the tri-state area. We have Metro-North train service, established communities, waterfront neighborhoods, vibrant downtowns, parks, restaurants, and an incredible variety of housing options.

From Rye, Harrison, and Mamaroneck along the Sound Shore to Scarsdale, White Plains, Yorktown, and communities along the Hudson River, buyers have plenty of different lifestyles to explore.

That proximity to NYC and the broader regional economy helps support demand for Westchester homes.

Historically, desirable properties in many of our communities have held their value relatively well, even when other housing markets experience more dramatic fluctuations. Of course, that doesn't mean prices can never decline. Every town, neighborhood, and property is different.

But it does explain why waiting for a major Westchester housing market crash may not be the best strategy.

The Right House Matters More Than Perfect Market Timing

Here's something I've learned from years of helping people buy and sell homes.

You can spend months, even years, trying to predict the perfect time to buy.

But when the right house comes along, in the right location, with the features you want and at a price you can comfortably afford, that's an opportunity worth considering.

You can potentially refinance a mortgage if rates decline in the future, although refinancing isn't guaranteed and comes with costs.

What you can't necessarily do is go back and purchase a home that someone else bought while you were waiting.

And that brings me to two buyers I'm currently working with.

I currently have two different buyers under contract in two different markets, and their experiences illustrate exactly what I'm talking about.

One of my buyers was ready to find their next home. Nothing high-pressure. Just an opportunity to get to know each other, talk about the areas they were considering, and discuss what they might want in their next home.

Should You Wait for Mortgage Rates to Drop Before Buying?

This is probably one of the most common questions I hear from Westchester County homebuyers.

And I understand it.

Mortgage rates directly affect your monthly payment and purchasing power. Even a relatively small change in rates can make a difference, especially given home prices in many Westchester communities.

But here's what I ask buyers to think about:

  • What happens if rates decline, but home prices rise because more buyers return to the market?
  • What happens if the property you could have purchased today attracts multiple offers six months from now?
  • And what if rates don't drop as much as you're expecting?

There are no guarantees. That's why I encourage buyers to focus on what they can control: their finances, their budget, their preparation, and their ability to recognize a good opportunity.

Start With a Mortgage Preapproval, Even If You're Not Ready to Buy

One of my first recommendations is to speak with a reputable mortgage professional and get preapproved. Not because I'm trying to rush you into buying a home. Quite the opposite.

I want you to understand what you can realistically afford before you start falling in love with properties that may not fit your budget.

A preapproval helps you:

  • Understand your approximate purchasing power.
  • Explore potential monthly mortgage payments.
  • Determine how much cash you'll need for a down payment and closing costs.
  • Identify financial issues you may want to address before purchasing.
  • Be better prepared to make an offer when the right home comes along.

Remember that property taxes can vary significantly from one Westchester community to another. Your monthly housing costs need to account for more than just principal and interest.

The goal is to buy a home you can comfortably afford, not simply the most expensive property a lender might approve.

Not Ready to Buy Yet? Here's What I Recommend Doing Now

When someone tells me they're thinking about purchasing a home in six months or even a year, I don't tell them they're making a mistake. I understand their thought process. Then I share mine.

There's a difference between not being ready to purchase and not being ready to explore your options. You don't need to start making offers tomorrow to benefit from learning about the market today.

Explore Westchester Neighborhoods on Your Own

One of my favorite suggestions is to spend some free weekends exploring the towns you're considering. Grab a coffee. Walk through downtown. Check out the parks, restaurants, shopping areas, and train stations. Get a feel for the different communities.

Rye has a very different atmosphere from White Plains. Mamaroneck offers a different experience from Scarsdale. And northern Westchester communities such as Yorktown and Somers may appeal to buyers looking for different property styles and settings.

The more familiar you become with the areas you're considering, the easier it becomes to narrow down what matters most to you.

Why Working With a Dedicated Buyer's Agent Matters in Westchester County

This is one of the most important pieces of advice I can give anyone considering purchasing a home: have your own real estate agent representing your interests.

I see buyers who find a property online and immediately contact the listing agent because they think that's the easiest way to arrange a showing. But there's something important they need to understand.

The listing agent represents the seller. Their fiduciary responsibilities are to the seller, not automatically to the buyer. That distinction matters.

A buyer's agent who represents you has a responsibility to advocate for your interests, help you evaluate properties, explain market conditions, and guide you through negotiations and the purchasing process.

In New York, agency relationships and any potential dual-agency arrangements must be properly disclosed, and buyers should understand whom an agent represents before proceeding.

So, Is 2026 a Good Time to Buy a House in Westchester County?

I believe it is, for buyers who are financially prepared and ready to take advantage of the right opportunity.

Does that mean everyone should rush out and buy a house? Absolutely not. If your finances aren't where they need to be, your employment situation is uncertain, or you aren't comfortable with the monthly cost of homeownership, waiting may be the smarter choice.

But if you're financially prepared and your primary reason for waiting is that you're hoping the market will suddenly become dramatically more favorable, I'd encourage you to reconsider that strategy.

Right now, some buyers may benefit from reduced competition, increased choices, and more room for negotiation than they would have had in a hotter market. Those conditions can vary significantly by town, price point, and property. And nobody knows exactly what next spring or next year will bring.

After all my years in this business, one thing I know is that the right home doesn't necessarily appear on the timeline you've created for yourself. Sometimes it shows up when you're not expecting it. Just ask my buyers who weren't planning to purchase until next year.
